## Why I Built This

I cook almost every day. It's my main creative outlet outside of work — the feedback loop is immediate, the materials are tangible, and nobody asks for stakeholder alignment on a pasta recipe.

Traditional recipe apps are frustrating. They can't answer "what if I don't have cilantro?" or "make this work in 30 minutes instead of an hour." Those are exactly the problems AI handles well — adaptation, substitution reasoning, contextual guidance.

But I also had a professional motivation: AI-powered features were becoming central to our product roadmap at Keela, and I wanted hands-on experience with conversational UI patterns before I had to lead design decisions about them.

## What I Designed

### Conversational Adaptation

Instead of static recipe cards, users can ask natural language questions: "Make this dairy-free," "I only have 30 minutes," "What can I substitute for shallots?" The AI responds with an adapted recipe and explains the reasoning behind each change.

The key insight was that **transparency is the trust mechanism**. Users accepted AI suggestions only when they could see the why. "Use coconut cream instead of heavy cream — it provides similar fat content for this sauce" lands differently than just swapping the ingredient silently.

This directly influenced how I later designed the automation preview feature at Keela. Same principle: make system logic visible to build confidence.

### Step-by-Step Cooking Mode

A focused view that breaks recipes into discrete actions with timing. Each step shows only what you need right now — ingredients, technique, timer. Designed for the reality of cooking: messy hands, divided attention, a phone propped up on the counter.

### Shareable Collections

Users curate recipe collections and share them as link-based cookbooks. I wanted the sharing experience to feel personal — like passing a handwritten recipe card to a friend, not forwarding a URL.

## Building End-to-End

React frontend, Supabase backend, OpenAI API for recipe adaptation. I focused heavily on prompt engineering — the AI needed to understand food science, not just swap ingredients mechanically.

Building end-to-end keeps me honest. I know what API latency feels like, what error states look like in reality (not just in a Figma frame), and where the gap between design intent and engineering reality tends to widen. That empathy makes me a better design leader.

## What I Took Away

- Designing for AI is fundamentally about trust, not intelligence
- The best AI interfaces augment human judgment rather than replacing it
- Personal projects are how I stay fluent in the materials my engineering partners work with every day

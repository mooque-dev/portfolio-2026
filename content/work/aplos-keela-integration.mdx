## The moment | The first thing the merged companies would ship together

Leadership read it as a test of the acquisition. The brief was a data sync between Keela's CRM and Aplos's accounting.

## What I found | Finance managers had been burned by data that moved silently

I talked to 12 nonprofit finance managers. Every spreadsheet, CSV export and double entry they kept existed because they'd been burned by data that moved silently. The problem to design for was trust.

The work also had to fit two engineering teams with different cadences and conventions, no shared components, and each side protective of its patterns. I spent the first two weeks in both teams' standups, mapping how each side thought about the same data, before designing anything.

## The call | Customers set up the sync and see every mapping first

I designed the setup so customers configure the sync themselves, and every data mapping is visible before anything moves. Most integrations hide this behind defaults.

The faster option was to map everything automatically. We didn't build it. Each default mapping is a guess about where a field belongs, and in accounting a silent wrong guess is the worst outcome. So defaults cover only the clear cases, every mapping is shown and editable, and an exceptions list surfaces anything that went wrong.

The work between the teams mattered as much as the screens. I ran the first joint design reviews between the two engineering teams, built the first shared integration components, and presented the integration framework to the executive team.

## What changed | The template for later cross-product work

It was the first feature the merged companies shipped together. The joint design reviews, the shared components and the integration framework all became the template for later cross-product work, and the components seeded the unified design system.

We didn't set a success number before launch, so what I can show is what shipped and what it started.

The integration also became one of the first pieces of ARK, the connected suite of Keela, Raisely and Aplos.

## Looking back | No success number, and what I'd measure now

We didn't set a success number up front, and that is the gap in this story. If I ran it again, I'd first count how many connected organizations still export CSVs or enter data twice, and track that down after launch. I'd also watch the sync success rate and the time to a first successful sync.

The number that must not rise is records posted to the wrong account. If it did, I'd pause the automatic sync and go back to review before posting.

What I keep from it: finance teams who have been burned before need to see their data before it moves.

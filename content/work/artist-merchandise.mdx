## Why I Do This

I studied Fine Arts before I studied design systems. That background keeps me connected to creative communities in a way that matters to me — not as charity, but as a practice that exercises completely different muscles than product work.

Most emerging artists who want to sell merchandise end up with clip art on a blank t-shirt because they can't afford a designer. I wanted to change that for at least a few people.

## How I Approached It

### Listening First

Each collaboration started with long conversations about the artist's practice, inspirations, and audience. I treated every engagement as a mini brand strategy project — understanding what the artist was actually trying to say before I designed anything. This is the same discovery approach I use with product stakeholders, just in a context where people think visually instead of in user stories.

### Systems, Not One-Offs

Rather than designing individual products, I built flexible visual systems for each artist: typography pairings, color palettes, layout templates, and production-ready files they could modify themselves. The goal was to build capability, not dependency.

This philosophy is the same one behind Orchid. The best systems are the ones people can use without you.

### Designing Within Real Constraints

Every design was created with manufacturing in mind — printing methods, material costs, minimum order quantities. Artists operating on tight budgets can't afford a beautiful design that's impractical to produce. Designing within constraints rather than ignoring them is something I care about in every context.

## What Came of It

- 4 artists launched merchandise lines generating real supplemental income
- The brand systems I designed kept being used long after our collaboration ended — artists extended them in ways I hadn't imagined
- I got better at mentorship and at explaining design decisions to people who aren't designers

## Why It Matters to My Day Job

The most rewarding moments in this work weren't when I delivered a polished design. They were when an artist took the system I'd built and made something new with it. That's the exact feeling I'm chasing with every design system I build.

If Orchid's components only produce what I imagined, I've failed. The point is to enable work I couldn't have predicted.

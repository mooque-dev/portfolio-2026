## Stakes | Context

Automation was the most requested feature on the roadmap, and competitors were winning deals on it. Nonprofit staff lose hours to thank-you emails, record updates and follow-ups. With the PM and CTO, I framed it as a platform capability, not a single feature.

## Research | The Research Surprise

I talked to 15 nonprofit operations managers, expecting complexity concerns. The top fear was simpler:

> "What if I accidentally email <mark class="pen" data-note="the fear that shaped the product">all 5,000</mark> of our donors?"

The problem wasn't complexity. It was confidence. Hands-on tests of HubSpot and Mailchimp, plus a review of four other tools, showed gaps in re-enrollment and contact-level control we could fill.

## Decisions | The Design

The easy version ships the workflow builder and stops. The hard problem was getting someone who had never trusted software with donor data to press activate, so every decision answers to that. The key one was **<mark class="pen" data-note="the answer to the fear">preview mode</mark>**: before anything goes live, you see exactly which contacts it will touch.

## Result | What Happened

Automation shipped in July 2021, five months after I joined Keela, as a paid add-on. It reached **30% adoption across 355 organizations** and contributed to a **13% ARR increase**.

<figure>
  <img src="/case-studies/automation-nonprofits/untitled-10.png" alt="Keela customer case study featuring automation, Love Quilt Project" />
  <figcaption>A customer case study featuring automation at the Love Quilt Project</figcaption>
</figure>

Building for confidence before capability is something I keep returning to.

## Reflection | What I Learned

It was first scoped at three weeks, too tight to interview, test and validate. With the PM and the lead designer, we made the case for more time, and the feature that shipped was loved by users and organizations alike.

It was also the first project where I accepted that collaboration makes a better product. I had thought every task was mine to finish alone, and I was afraid to ask for help. It's one of my proudest projects, and it couldn't have been built without the people credited below.

> "After only a month on the job we handed him one of Keela's most complex features to research and design. What an accomplishment to see Automation finally being released today."

Carolyn, Product Manager, on release day.

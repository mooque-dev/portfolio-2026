## The graveyard problem

Everyone already saves things. Bookmarks, Instagram collections, twelve open tabs, a Notes app full of links you meant to read. Saving was never the problem. The problem is that almost none of it comes back. A save-it-later tool is only worth the odds that you return to it, and for most of them those odds are close to zero.

So I designed Mooze around the return, not the save.

## What I decided

The save is the cheap part, so it got the least attention. Everything you keep, a video, an article, a book, a song, becomes one kind of thing: an item. A Spotify link and a PDF land in the same searchable place instead of scattering across five apps. The AI pass exists for a single reason, that a thing you saved and never reopened is worthless, so it distills an entry down to why you kept it, not just what it is.

And it is signed-in, on a real database, because the premise collapses if it cannot hold years. That was the deliberate tradeoff: the heavier "built to keep" version over a quick, disposable one. Inspiration only compounds if it survives.

## Why it's here

Mooze is where the vault idea on this site started. Same belief underneath both: the value is never in collecting, it is in whether you can find it again.

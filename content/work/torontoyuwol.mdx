## The moment | Forty volunteers, two languages, one group chat

In 2025 I joined an amateur musical company, well outside my comfort zone. It
had months of rehearsals ahead, forty people across cast, production, and
promotion teams, two languages, and everything coordinated through group chat.

## What I found | A spreadsheet nobody opened and a chat nobody could search

I found the operational chaos every volunteer group knows. Everyone was
committed, but the only tools were a spreadsheet nobody opened and a chat nobody
could search. Who was coming to a practice lived in a thread. Rehearsing a
translated show meant half the room worked from Korean and half from English.
And forty people sharing costs is its own coordination problem.

## The call | One small tool for each named annoyance

I started building small tools, each for one annoyance. Adoption was never
mandated.

**Scheduling and attendance.** A shared calendar of practices and performances,
with attendance tracked per date, so you can see who's in the room at a glance.
People pick their team and their language; the whole thing runs in Korean and
English because the company does.

**A bilingual script reader.** The reader keeps the Korean and English scripts
in one place. [Try the script reader demo](https://torontoyuwol-brown.vercel.app/demo.html).

**Bill splitting.** A small app splits a bill and keeps the math out of the
group chat.

The people using these tools were non-technical, and I saw them every week. They
told me directly what was broken, and I iterated weekly on that feedback. When
the stage manager walked over to say the attendance view confused her, that
told me more than an analytics dashboard would have.

## What changed | Used through the run, and a show that sold 350 tickets

The show went up in August 2026 and sold 350 tickets. The script reader was used
daily through the run, which made it the most-loved thing I built for them. The
tools spread because each one removed a specific, named annoyance, and the people
it served watched it get better every week.

## Looking back | Season two

Season two is underway, and so is the next round of tools.

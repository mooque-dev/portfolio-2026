## An app with exactly one user

Most travel apps are built for everyone, which is why they serve no one in particular: accounts, feeds, booking upsells, features for trips you are not taking. Wayfind had one user, me, and one trip: London, Frankfurt, Berlin, and Copenhagen, built around design studios and jazz. That constraint is the whole reason it works. No committee, no personas, no imagined general case. Just the actual thing I needed while walking out of a train station.

## What I decided

So it is designed for the hand, not the desk. Mobile-first, bottom nav, one thumb, because that is the real posture of travel. The trip reads as a timeline instead of a pile of bookings, so "what is next" is always one glance away, which on the ground is the only question you have. Logging an expense takes two taps, because any friction at the moment of spending means it never gets logged and the budget quietly turns into fiction. Time-sensitive alerts sit on top; everything else gets out of the way.

## What it taught me

Designing for one real use, honestly, beats designing for an imagined everyone. You can feel the difference in the result, and I have carried that bias back into product work since.
